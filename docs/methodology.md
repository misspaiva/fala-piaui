# Methodology — Fala, Piauí

## Purpose

Fala, Piauí is an initial, manually curated dataset of words and expressions associated with Portuguese as spoken in Piauí, Brazil.

The project documents situated language rather than attempting to define a single, fixed, or statistically representative “Piauí dialect.”

## Initial scope

Version 0.1 and version 0.2 begin with a small collection of expressions selected from the author's lived linguistic context in Southern Piauí, in a border area with Pernambuco and Ceará.

The dataset does not claim to represent all of Piauí. Vocabulary, pronunciation, meaning, and frequency may vary substantially across Northern, Central, Southern, rural, urban, and border-area contexts within the state.

Future regional collections must be documented as separate scopes. Records from one locality, speaker, family, or border region must not be treated as evidence that all people in Piauí use an expression in the same way.

## Inclusion criteria

An expression may be included when the author can provide:

1. A contextual meaning in Brazilian Portuguese;
2. An original example sentence;
3. A description of where or how the expression was encountered;
4. A source type, such as personal memory, family speech, observation, reference work, or community contribution;
5. At least one note about uncertainty, variation, or contextual limits.

## Geographic claims

Piauí is the initial geographic focus.

An expression may be associated with a city, area, or broader region only when that association is supported by the available record. When geographic distribution is uncertain, the dataset uses cautious language such as “to be confirmed.”

A record from one speaker, family, city, or community must not be treated as evidence that all people in Piauí or Northeast Brazil use an expression in the same way.

## Translation approach

Expressions remain in their original Portuguese forms.

English fields provide contextual explanations rather than claiming that every expression has a direct English equivalent. When no direct translation exists, the dataset documents approximation and limitation.

## Sensitive and socially contextual language

Some expressions may relate to social identity, rurality, accent, class, education, humor, stigma, or prejudice.

For such entries, the dataset records tone, speaker-target relationship, possible harm, and contextual variation whenever possible. It does not reproduce stereotypes as facts about people or communities.

## Health and clinical boundaries

Words that describe feelings, sensations, confusion, discomfort, or behavior are documented as everyday language uses.

The dataset does not use expressions as clinical, medical, psychological, or diagnostic labels unless a future record is explicitly based on an appropriate source and clearly identified as such.

## Confidence levels

- `initial`: recorded from an early observation or memory and still needs confirmation;
- `medium`: supported by repeated observation or a contextual reference, but variation remains unclear;
- `high`: the author has strong direct knowledge of the recorded use.

A high confidence level for a recorded use does not automatically mean high confidence about the expression's distribution across all of Piauí or Brazil.

## Future validation

Future versions may include:

- contextual reference sources;
- contributions from additional speakers;
- locality and generation comparisons;
- review of translation choices;
- documentation of disagreement and variation;
- transparent version history.

## Current limitations

Version 0.1 is small, manually curated, and rooted in one author's experience.

It should not be used as a definitive dictionary, a population-level linguistic survey, a tool for identifying a person's origin, or a basis for high-stakes decisions.
